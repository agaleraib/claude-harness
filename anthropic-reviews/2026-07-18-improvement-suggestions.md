## 4. Enrich `spec-planner` Discovery Phase with the four-quadrant unknowns framework (Fable 5 field guide)

**Source:** [A field guide to Claude Fable 5: Finding your unknowns — claude.com/blog, 2026-07-06](https://claude.com/blog/a-field-guide-to-claude-fable-finding-your-unknowns) by Thariq Shihipar (Anthropic MTS). Positions Fable 5 as "the first model where the quality of work is bottlenecked by the ability to clarify its unknowns" and frames unknowns into four categories:

- **Known knowns** — what you already put in the prompt.
- **Known unknowns** — things you know are unresolved.
- **Unknown knowns** — implicit taste and assumptions you'd recognize if you saw them.
- **Unknown unknowns** — missing context you haven't even thought to ask about.

The guide advocates for **iterative discovery** — before, during, and after implementation — as the practical skill for Fable 5-era prompting.

### Why it's relevant

The harness's `spec-planner` agent already has a Discovery Phase (`.claude/agents/spec-planner.md:29`) that:
- Mandates unknowns focus: "Ask 1-4 targeted questions per round using `AskUserQuestion`. Focus on the biggest unknowns first." (line 36)
- Uses `AskUserQuestion` — a first-party clarification-elicitation tool.
- Runs up to 3 rounds (line 44).

Grepping the repo:
- `spec-planner.md:36, 62, 389` — three "unknowns" references. All frame unknowns as things-to-elicit, not categorized by knowability.
- No references to "known unknowns", "unknown unknowns", or the four-quadrant terminology (`grep -rn "known unknowns\|unknown unknowns\|four-quadrant" .` returns nothing).

The field guide's contribution is **categorization**: telling the interviewer which unknowns to hunt for at which stage. "Unknown knowns" (implicit taste) and "unknown unknowns" (missing context you haven't thought to ask about) are the hardest categories, and they're exactly the ones spec-planner is likeliest to miss.

### Why this is `defer`, not `apply`

1. **`spec-planner`'s current framing already works.** The 2026-04-19 §5 evals scaffolding is still `DEFERRED`, so the harness has no measured-failure evidence that discovery is missing categories. The harness pins Opus 4.7 (soon 4.8 per §1) — different capability regime. Wait until the Fable-5 tier bump (§2) before applying Fable-5-specific discovery patterns.
3. **The framework is well-known outside Anthropic.** Rumsfeld's original four-quadrant version is 20+ years old and appears in software-engineering and product-planning literature broadly. It's not a novel Anthropic contribution the way "loop engineering" (§3) is — the value-add of citing Anthropic specifically is smaller.
4. **Trigger to revisit:** if `/planning-loop`'s acceptance-strictness scanner (`skills/planning-loop/lib/`) ever surfaces "spec had missing context that discovery didn't catch" as a repeat pattern, promote this § to apply and add the four-quadrant vocabulary as an explicit checklist.

## 5. Cross-reference **How we contain Claude across products** as evidence for the `sandcastle` vs `worktree` runner split in `AGENTS.md` "Loop protocol"

**Source:** [How we contain Claude across products — anthropic.com/engineering, 2026-05-28](https://www.anthropic.com/engineering/how-we-contain-claude) by Max McGuinness et al. Detailed engineering post on how the three Anthropic-hosted Claude surfaces (claude.ai, Claude Code, Claude Cowork) sandbox agent execution: **claude.ai** uses gVisor + ephemeral filesystem, **Claude Code** uses Seatbelt (macOS) and Bubblewrap (Linux), **Claude Cowork** runs a full VM (Apple Virtualization on macOS, HCS on Windows). Core thesis: *"rather than supervising what the agent does, supervise what it's able to do by enforcing access boundaries."* Includes concrete incident reports, measured failure rates, and architectural trade-offs.

### Why it's relevant

The harness's `run-loop` skill declares two runners:
- **`sandcastle`** (default) — container-isolated, "the OS is its security boundary" (`AGENTS.md:107`, `skills/run-loop/SKILL.md:45`).
- **`worktree`** — native on host, "the OS is NOT the boundary, so the worktree lane carries the safety guardrails below" (`AGENTS.md:107`).

Both are described in-repo without reference to the vendor's canonical containment vocabulary. Grepping:
- `sandcastle` appears in 6+ files (design docs, protocol, SKILL.md).
- `worktree` in 15+ files (protocol + engine).
- **Zero references** to the vendor's containment post or terminology (gVisor, Seatbelt, Bubblewrap, HCS, egress control) outside of the loop protocol's "default-deny outbound egress" phrase (`AGENTS.md:113`).

The vendor's post gives the harness's runner split a citable evidence baseline: the "sandcastle vs worktree" decision maps onto Anthropic's own "container-boundary vs native" trade-off. ## 6. Adopt the "codebase-scale migration under Dynamic Workflows" pattern
